# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

static HTML/CSS/JS first; framework decision deferred until a hero direction is picked.

## Users

One person: the owner (Jacob), using it as a personal command center. Situation: opening the app at the start of the day or between tasks to see what's on, what needs doing, and how each area of life is tracking. No other users or roles.

## Product Purpose

HQ is an all-in-one personal dashboard/checklist for every practical aspect of the owner's life: calendar, finances, workout schedule, study schedule, health. Success = the owner opens it, immediately knows what today holds and where each area stands, and feels welcomed rather than nagged.

## Positioning

A single private surface where all life areas meet, tuned to one person's actual routines, rather than five separate apps with generic defaults.

## Operating Context

Desktop browser primarily (local server during design; deployment undecided). Morning check-in and quick mid-day glances. Data sources undecided: manual entry for now; integrations (e.g. Google Calendar, Todoist) are possible later but not committed.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Modules: calendar/schedule, finances, workouts, study plan, health.
- Home screen answers "what's on today" plus one status per area.
- Terminology: "areas" for the five modules.
- Undecided: data storage, sync/integrations, mobile support depth, auth.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: HQ.
- Tone: welcoming and insightful; never nagging or gamified.
- Visual references on hand (screenshots of dashboards the owner likes) are guidance, not binding: `C:\Users\jpstu\Desktop\websiteinspo\`.
- Five aesthetic directions explicitly requested for exploration: clean, friendly, minimal, techy, classical.

## Evidence on Hand

No real data yet; all dashboard content in mockups is synthetic and must be labeled/replaced. No logo. No testimonials or claims apply (single-user product).

## Product Principles

1. Today first: the next hour outranks the quarter.
2. One number per area, and it means something.
3. Welcoming over demanding: surface progress before shortfalls.
4. Everything in one place; no module is a silo.
5. Private by default.
